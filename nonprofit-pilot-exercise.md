# Nonprofit AI Workflow Pilot Planning Exercise

Joseph Johnson | SRS portfolio | Prepared October 15, 2026

**Project type: Self-directed simulation inspired by my preliminary nonprofit Copilot feasibility concept.** The organization is fictional and unnamed. Dates are relative weeks, costs and staffing are illustrative, and all progress, approvals, and events below are simulated. No real deployment, test execution, client acceptance, savings, or contract result is claimed.

## Project charter

**Scenario:** A nonprofit wants to explore AI assistance for a monthly board-report draft and meeting notes converted into a task list. Its small team needs a controlled pilot before considering wider adoption.

**Objective:** Produce and evaluate sample board-report and task-list workflows within eight weeks, with a documented handoff and a go/no-go recommendation for further testing.

**Scope:** Requirements, feature and license verification, sample input preparation, workflow design, authorized test-environment configuration if available, human review, pilot feedback, and handoff documentation.

**Exclusions:** Production deployment, autonomous scheduling or messaging, donor or beneficiary records, PHI, confidential client data, integrations with live business systems, and an organization-wide rollout. Other ideas from the original concept remain future candidates.

**Success criteria:** Two documented workflow examples; all mandatory acceptance checks completed; no unresolved critical privacy or access defects; a reviewer-confirmed task list; a board-report draft with no unsupported figures; and a documented decision on the next phase. These are targets, not achieved results.

**Roles:** Joseph as exercise planner and capture/consulting lead; a fictional nonprofit sponsor as decision authority; a designated tenant administrator for access and licensing; and two fictional staff reviewers. One person may hold multiple roles. No actual staffing commitment is implied.

**Assumptions and constraints:** Eight-week window; a notional 100-hour labor allowance; synthetic inputs only; timely administrator participation. Tool access is assumed available by the end of week 2 in the baseline. Current licensing, feature availability, and tenant controls must be verified. If paid capacity or licensing is required, the sponsor must separately approve a quote before access or spending. This exercise makes no claim that existing licenses cover the work.

## Change request CR 01

**Exercise event at the end of week 2:** Required tool access is delayed by two weeks, becoming available at the end of week 4. The week-8 finish and 100-hour labor allowance remain fixed.

**Impact:** Configuration and tool-based walkthroughs cannot start as planned. The original two-week pilot window is lost. Synthetic sample preparation and manual workflow rehearsal can continue without tenant access.

| Option | Schedule and effort effect | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extend the finish to week 10 | Retains baseline pilot duration; extra coordination must be estimated | Conflicts with fixed finish |
| Keep week 8 and reduce pilot breadth | Configure in week 5, run focused walkthroughs in week 6, validate and hand off in week 7 | Less reviewer exposure and no evidence of sustained adoption |
| Defer tool-based pilot | Finish an offline design package by week 8 | Meets planning date but does not evaluate tool behavior |

**Recommendation:** Keep week 8 and reduce pilot breadth. Retain both workflow examples but limit pilot exposure to one focused session per workflow. Preserve all privacy, access, accuracy, and minimum acceptance checks. Reallocate 8 pilot hours into 4 configuration hours, 2 validation hours, and 2 coordination hours. Total remains 100 hours.

**Simulated decision record:** For this exercise, assume the fictional sponsor conditionally approves the narrower pilot. Conditions: administrator authorization before tool use; no unapproved nonlabor spending; all mandatory checks retained; and explicit acceptance of reduced feedback coverage. This is an exercise assumption, not a real approval. If access slips again or the checks cannot be completed, hold the pilot and report incomplete validation.

## Retrospective and outcome

**Exercise outcome:** Completed a planning package with a baseline, a delay scenario, options, a conditional simulated decision, revised effort and schedule, risks, and acceptance criteria. No real organization implemented this plan, and no tests were run as part of this written exercise.

**Planning lessons:** Resolve access and licensing early; separate delivery confidence from optimistic assumptions; preserve validation when narrowing scope; document accepted limits; and follow up on named actions. These are lessons from the planning exercise, not observed client outcomes.

**What I would change for a real engagement:** Confirm staffing and cost quotes, obtain actual sponsor decisions, run a small authorized pilot, collect evidence against each criterion, and measure reviewer effort and output quality before proposing expansion.
